Alternative Title: Poorly-researched Vibes-based Predictions for the Next Ten Years based on my Biased and Libbed-out Information Diet; a.k.a. Complete Speculation

Writing out my predictions for the future has helped me realize that I have not been living my life according to my beliefs. I live as if the world is coming to an end tomorrow, because I am a pessimist, yet my rational brain believes human civilization has at least 5-10 good years left before a catastrophe happens. Five years is plenty of time to live a good life and support some medium-term goals.

It's in moments like these that I am reminded about a parable from Dostoevsky's novel *The Idiot*. A man is in line awaiting his execution by firing squad.

> He had about five minutes to live, not more. He said those five minutes seemed like an endless time to him, an enormous wealth. It seemed to him that in those five minutes he would live so many lives that there was no point yet in thinking about his last moment... he said that nothing was more oppresive for him at that moment than the constant thought: 'What if I were not to die! What if life were given back to me—what infinity!... I'd turn each minute into a whole age, I'd lose nothing, I'd reckon up each minute separately, I'd let nothing be wasted!'.

The story concludes with the man indeed being spared at the last minute, yet after the event, "he didn't live that way at all and lost many, many minutes".

Everyone knows that they should not take their time for granted, but how many people truly live that way?

3. Continual learning (similar to human learning on the job) will not occur until 2030+
4. One/few-shot realistic video and voice cloning will be achieved by 2028. Scammers can clone anyone with a few seconds of video.
5. Anthropic and OpenAI go public and their valuation collapses within a few years thereafter.
6. "AGI" is achieved sometime around 2032 (for me continual learning is a precondition for AGI)
7. Despite AGI being achieved, AI companies largely go bust. The "AI trade" collapses.
8. Mathematician employment will stay largely the same, their work will just suck more. The fear of layoffs in academia is seen to have been overblown.
9. Direct layoffs due to automation by AI are rare. The primary mechanism for a permanent reduction in the workforce will be people laid off due to macro conditions, and companies avoiding re-hiring by replacement with AI.
10. This causes youth/entry-level unemployment, especially in high-status industries, to surge.
11. Humanoid robots are largely seen to have been overhyped. Robotics turns out to be much harder than expected. We will not achieve general intelligence operating in a physical manner by 2036.

\* I'd be fairly convinced we had AGI for software if an AI is able to autonomously use Photoshop to create a high-quality painting according to instructions, without a reference, and in the same manner that a human would do it (drawing an outline layer, etc.)
